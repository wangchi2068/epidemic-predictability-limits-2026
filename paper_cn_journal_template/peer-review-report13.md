## 1. Summary

The manuscript proposes an analytical framework for the *predictability horizon* of epidemic forecasts: the lead time beyond which the relative mean squared error of an exponential-growth forecast exceeds a preset tolerance τ. The transmission process is modelled as a negative-binomial branching process with reproduction number R, dispersion parameter k, and initial incidence I₀, with the estimator R̂ propagated lognormally.

The theoretical section derives an additive decomposition of the squared relative error into an intrinsic-stochasticity term CV²(h) and a parameter-extrapolation term P(h), and defines the horizon h\* as the largest lead time satisfying CV²(h) + P(h) ≤ τ² (Lemmas 1–3, Theorem 1, Corollary 1). Supporting results cover strict monotonicity of the error in h (Theorem 2), a reflecting-boundary diffusion approximation to quasi-stationary behaviour in finite populations with critical-slowing-down scaling t_relax ~ O(N^(1/2)) (Theorem 3, Corollary 2), a Cramér–Rao lower bound on the relative variance of R̂ under a negative-binomial likelihood giving a required independent-cluster count C_req = (1/R + 1/k)/s² (Theorem 4), and variance-accumulation laws for time-varying transmission, including an AR(1) extension yielding cubic divergence at the unit root (Theorems 5 and 5R, Corollary 3).

The empirical section applies the framework to seven phases of three respiratory pathogens in the United States (COVID-19 Delta, Omicron, JN.1; influenza 2022–23 and 2024–25; RSV 2024–25 and 2025–26), each parameterised from a five-week window by weekly log-linear OLS with a parametric bootstrap (B = 2,000, seed 20260807). The headline empirical claims are: theoretical horizons of 7.9–21.2 weeks for the non-RSV phases; uncapped RSV roots of 41.5–67.8 weeks that are explicitly labelled model artifacts because they exceed the 16–20-week seasonal span; CV² contributing at most 0.74% of τ²; a Cramér–Rao "violation ratio" of 2.46 for RSV 2025–26; a four-term error accounting in which modelled mechanistic terms explain only 0.4%–48.4% of observed error and unmodelled structural residuals account for 51.2%–99.6%; and a pseudo-prospective rolling evaluation in which skill decays to a naive persistence baseline within 1.8–5.3 weeks. The discussion maps four tolerance levels onto public-health decision scenarios and adds a section on health equity.

This is a revised submission. In addition to the manuscript, this review assessed the authors' `Response_to_Reviewers.md` point by point against the current source, and independently re-derived the theory and recomputed the reported numbers from the JSON artifacts in the author repository. No repository script was executed; all recomputation was done by reimplementing the formulas as printed in the manuscript, in an independent session, using field values parsed directly from `reports/*.json`. Where this review reports a numerical discrepancy, both sides of the comparison are traceable to a line of `main.tex` or a field of a deposited artifact.

## 2. Strengths

**S1. The core mathematics is correct.** Every derivation on the main analytical path was re-derived independently, symbolically and numerically, and all of it holds: the Lemma 1 variance recursion Var(Z_h) = I₀σ²R^(h−1)(R^h − 1)/(R − 1); the Lemma 2 closed form for CV²(h) together with its h = 1 special case and its R → 1 limit; the Lemma 3 lognormal expression P(h;s) = e^(2h²s²) − 2e^(h²s²/2) + 1 and its (hs)² expansion; the Theorem 2 monotonicity argument via the auxiliary function g(y) = (y^h₂ − 1)² − (y^h₁ − 1)²; the Theorem 3 quasi-stationary density π(I) ∝ I⁻¹e^(c₁I − c₂I²), which follows from the zero-flux condition with the stated coefficients; the Theorem 4 Fisher information I₁(R) = k/[R(R + k)] and the resulting bound; the sample-size constants 6.18, 231, and 1,428; and the Theorem 5R stationary autocovariance sum with its s_e²h³/3 divergence at φ = 1. In a paper carrying six theorems, three lemmas, and three corollaries, an error-free algebraic core is a real accomplishment and it means the problems identified below are repairable rather than fatal.

## 4. Detailed comments

### Major comments

**Major 1 — The dimensional error in `E_drift`: the code squares a quantity that is already a variance. [verified]**

*Location:* formula at main.tex:616; implementation at `scripts_v2/error_budget_v2.py:126` with `drift_volatility()` at lines 70–84; affected artifact `reports/table4_budget.json` (all 12 cells); affected prose at main.tex:62, :660, :790, :1101.

*Problem:* main.tex:616 defines E_drift(h) = h_gen · v̂², where v̂² is "the residual variance of weekly log-growth increments about their 4-week centred moving average" — that is, v̂² is itself a variance. The function at line 84 returns `np.var(resid, ddof=1)`, assigns it to a variable named `v_drift`, and line 126 then computes `e_drift = h_gen * v_drift ** 2`, i.e. h_gen · v̂⁴. Recomputing both candidate formulas from the JSON's own `v_drift` and `h_gen` fields confirms the code path: for Delta h = 1 the JSON value 2.018e−06 matches h_gen·v_drift² (2.0175e−06), not h_gen·v_drift (1.7332e−03); the same holds for flu22 h = 1 and Omicron h = 8. The variable name is the likely origin of the bug: it suggests v, but holds v².

*Why it matters:* since v̂² ≪ 1, the extra squaring suppresses the drift term by two to three orders of magnitude, and every share in the JSON is computed from the suppressed value. Recomputing the closure residual under the *printed* formula gives structural-residual shares of 99.2% (Delta h = 4), 12.3% (Omicron h = 8, versus the 51.25% that is the abstract's lower bound), and −54.2%, −297.5%, and −138.2% for flu22 h = 1, rsv24 h = 2, and rsv25 h = 2. Under the manuscript's own formula, the drift term alone exceeds the observed total error in three of twelve configurations, and the advertised 51.2%–99.6% range does not exist; the recomputed range is −297.5% to 99.2%. Either the code is wrong, in which case the abstract's number is wrong, or the printed formula is wrong, in which case the theory and the implementation have diverged. Note also that main.tex:618 promises not to clip negative residuals and to treat them as diagnostic signals — yet all twelve JSON cells are positive, which indicates the authors have only ever observed the buggy output.

*Action:* decide which quantity `drift_volatility()` is meant to return and make the name match. If it returns a variance, line 126 becomes `e_drift = h_gen * v_drift`; if the squaring is intended, line 84 should return `np.std(...)`. Then rerun the chain and rewrite the entire four-term accounting section, Table 4, Figure 6, the abstract at main.tex:62, the English abstract at :1101, and the conclusion at :790. If negative residuals appear after the fix, report them as promised at main.tex:618 rather than suppressing them, and add a closure-sign column to Table 4.

**Major 6 — The implementation of C_req contains an extra factor R², so the headline violation ratio 2.46 should be 1.48. [verified]**

*Location:* formula at main.tex:533; implementation at `scripts_v2/pipeline.py:256–262`; `reports/crb_analysis.json`; abstract at main.tex:62 and :1101.

*Problem:* main.tex:533 prints C_req = (1/R + 1/k)/s². The code computes `s_rel = rec["s_gen"] / rec["R_gen"]` and then `c_req = (1.0/R_gen + 1.0/k_agg) / s_rel**2`. But `s_gen` is already a *relative* standard error — Table 2's note at main.tex:561 defines s = Δ_g · SE(b_week) as the relative standard error on the log scale, and Table 2's s column is that quantity. Dividing by R again yields C_req = R²(1/R + 1/k)/s². Computing both variants from the Table 2 parameters and comparing against `crb_analysis.json` confirms that all seven phases match the R² version: rsv24 gives 4,882 under the printed formula versus 8,694 as reported; rsv25 gives 13,785 versus 22,986; Delta 4,474 versus 6,829; flu22 2,637 versus 3,849.

*Why it matters:* under the manuscript's own formula the rsv25 ratio is 1.48, not 2.46. The qualitative conclusion (ratio above one) survives; the number printed in both abstracts does not. This bug was never exposed by the build chain because `crb_analysis()` is defined *after* `if __name__ == "__main__": main()` in `pipeline.py` and is never called — no script in the repository writes `crb_analysis.json`, so `make_all.py` does not regenerate it. The three consistency assertions on this artifact read only the JSON's own fields and place no constraint on whether it agrees with the printed formula.

*Action:* decide whether `s_gen` is relative (as Table 2's note says) and drop the extra division, or correct the formula at main.tex:533 to include R². Update main.tex:533 and both abstracts. Move `crb_analysis()` above `__main__` and wire it into `make_all.py`. Add an assertion that C_req equals the value recomputed from `table2_params.json` under the printed formula.

**Major 7 — The Cramér–Rao comparison substitutes an OLS slope standard error into a bound derived for a negative-binomial likelihood. [judgement, with a verified factual basis]**

*Location:* Theorem 4 at main.tex:372–391; empirical application at :533; Appendix D at :1015; `reports/crb_analysis.json`.

*Problem:* Theorem 4 bounds the relative variance of an unbiased maximum-likelihood estimator of R constructed from C independently observed transmission clusters under a negative-binomial offspring likelihood. The s that is substituted into C_req = (1/R + 1/k)/s² comes from a different estimation procedure entirely: per main.tex:561 it is the standard error of a log-linear OLS slope fitted to five aggregated weekly points with df = 3. The mismatch has three layers. The observational unit differs (C independent clusters versus five highly autocorrelated aggregate time points). The information source differs (the negative-binomial MLE extracts information about R from the dispersion of the offspring distribution, whose Fisher information scales with C; the OLS slope extracts it from a temporal trend, whose precision depends on point count and leverage). And the manuscript itself concedes at main.tex:533 that weekly aggregation and laboratory testing "artificially deflate the sample residual" — which, since s² sits in the denominator, artificially *inflates* C_req; the reported 2.46 may be an arithmetic amplification of that smoothing rather than a genuine information-theoretic shortfall.

A corroborating signal: the ratios across the seven phases in `crb_analysis.json` span 0.006 (Omicron) to 2.461 (rsv25), a range of roughly 400×. A genuine information-sufficiency diagnostic should not vary by that much within one surveillance system; the spread looks more like variation in how heavily each series is smoothed.

**Minor 7 — The cross-scale equivalence claim is too strong.** main.tex:214 states that solving the horizon equation directly in weekly units and solving in generation units then converting give "exactly the same" calendar-week horizon. **Minor 8 — The estimator is described as both unbiased-normal and biased-lognormal.** Lemma 3 at main.tex:249 assumes log R̂ ~ N(log R, s²), which implies E[R̂] = Re^(s²/2) > R; Figure 1 at :139 labels R̂ as an unbiased sliding-window estimator distributed N(R, δR²); Theorem 4 at :373 requires an unbiased estimator; and the proof of Theorem 2 at :914 assumes E[Y] = 1. The normal and lognormal assumptions cannot both hold, and the entire closed form for P(h) depends on the lognormal one. **Minor 9 — Theorem 5's conditioning is never used in its proof.** The statement at main.tex:398 conditions on non-extinction, Var(log Z_h | Z_h > 0), but the proof at :403 and Appendix E at :1034 compute the unconditional variance, adding Var(Ση_t) = hv² and Var(Σξ_t) ≈ h/k directly. Conditioning on non-extinction is a non-trivial change of measure that affects both terms and is never invoked; the log(Z_h + 1) regularisation addresses well-definedness, not the conditional distribution. Two smaller points in the same proof: Appendix E:1034 replaces 1/E[Z_t] by 0 after using a *conditional* variance, mixing conditional variance with unconditional mean, where strictness would require ΣE[1/Z_t], and by Jensen's inequality E[1/Z_t] ≥ 1/E[Z_t] with the gap largest on near-extinction paths — exactly the paths conditioning was meant to handle; and the independence of {η_t} and {ξ_t} is used in the addition but never assumed in (A1)–(A4) or in the theorem. *Action:* either drop the conditioning from the statement and restrict to the E[Z_h] ≫ 1 regime, or carry the conditioning through the proof; state the independence assumption explicitly.

## 5. Overall recommendation and score

**Recommendation: major revision.**

**Score: 4 / 10.**

The theoretical core of this paper is sound. Every derivation on the main path was re-derived independently and none contains an algebraic error, which in a manuscript carrying six theorems, three lemmas, and three corollaries is a genuine achievement. The data are frozen with verifiable hashes, the reproducibility parameters agree line by line between manuscript and code, the previous round's retractions were executed without residue, a hallucinated citation was identified and removed by the authors themselves, and the manuscript discloses at least nine facts that work against its own narrative. Several items from the previous round — the generation-interval labelling, the Table 2/3 explanation, the data-chain documentation, the single-sourcing of Figure 7 — were delivered properly. This is not a careless submission, and the authors' own qualifications are frequently better written than their conclusions.

The score is nevertheless low, and it is set by a single fact: **the paper's empirical results cannot currently be reproduced from the paper's own deposited materials, and the quality mechanism built in response to the previous round is structurally incapable of detecting this.** Table 4 disagrees with its own generating artifact in both row set and magnitude; the artifact is itself produced by code containing a dimensional error; the P column is low by exactly tenfold in nine of twelve entries; the CV² columns of Tables 3 and 4 are unreproducible; and the Cramér–Rao implementation carries an extra R² that turns a headline 2.46 into 1.48. Two of the three numbers a reader is most likely to quote from the abstract — the 51.2%–99.6% residual share and the 2.46 violation ratio — do not survive recomputation. Meanwhile Figure 3 is entirely blank, so the only near-critical theorem has no numerical evidence in the submitted document at all.

The reason none of this was caught is the finding this review considers most important. `main.tex` contains no `\input` statement; the generated table bodies never enter the compilation; and both table assertions in the consistency script validate that non-compiled file. Net assertion coverage over the printed tables is zero. This explains how the response letter can sincerely claim that a table is generated from JSON while the printed table comes from somewhere else entirely, and it means the response letter's central promise — a single source of truth enforced by a failing build — is not currently in effect. The letter's line numbers, which point almost uniformly to the wrong content, are a symptom of the same underlying disconnection between what is claimed and what is checked.

A rejection would not be the right call. The problems are concentrated in the bridge from theory to data, not in the theory; every one of them is repairable; and the pattern across findings — hand-typed manuscript right and generated artifact wrong in some places, the reverse in others — indicates two data streams that have run in parallel without ever being reconciled, rather than any attempt to mislead. Once the streams are joined, most of the numerical findings above resolve mechanically.

**To reach acceptance-with-minor-revision, the following must be completed.** First, connect the build: replace the hand-typed table bodies in `main.tex` with `\input` of generated fragments, redirect the consistency assertions at the compiled source, extend them to Tables 3, 4, and 6 and to the 0.74% figure and the Corollary 1 error domain, upgrade the figure check from existence to non-emptiness, and wire `crb_analysis.json`, `verify_t3.json`, and `verify_t4.json` back into `make_all.py` so that one command genuinely rebuilds everything. Second, fix the four numerical defects — the `E_drift` squaring, the C_req R², the tenfold P column, and the I₀ definition conflict — then regenerate Table 4, Figure 6, the abstract, and the conclusion, reporting negative closure residuals honestly if they appear, as main.tex:618 already promises. Third, repair Figure 3 and verify the four ratios it is supposed to support. Fourth, correct the rounding direction at main.tex:291, restore the 1/k term in Theorem 5R or withdraw the generalisation claim, and either prove or cite the spectral gap in Appendix C. Fifth, disclose what is currently withheld: the null local-linear crossings and the reversed directional reading, the per-wave `n_boot_ok` values, the ceiling truncation of k and its effect on the 0.74% figure, the extrapolation factors in Table 2, and the full-domain 48.6% error for Corollary 1. Sixth, bring the abstract into line with the body's own qualifications — insert 探索性, replace "actual operational skill" with pseudo-prospective finalized-vintage wording, name the baseline, and align the strength of both abstracts. Seventh, cite Wesselkamp et al. and Castro et al. and state the increment relative to each.

Two closing observations for the authors. The qualifications in this manuscript are written better than its conclusions; the fastest route to a stronger paper is to bring the abstract and conclusion down to the standard already set at main.tex:613, :670, :703, and :706, rather than defending the stronger language. And several criticisms in this review rest on information the authors themselves chose to publish — the ceiling hit, the shared-window dependence, the interval widths, the vintage caveat. That is what the transparency is for, and it should not be reduced in the next round.

## Scope and limitations of this review

Stated so that the authors can judge how much weight each finding carries.

**What was verified directly.** All findings marked [verified] rest on three-way agreement between the manuscript text at a stated line, a field value parsed from a deposited artifact, and an independent recomputation. The recomputations were performed by reimplementing the formulas as printed in `main.tex`, in a separate Python session, using values read from `reports/*.json`. This makes the "formula versus artifact" comparisons third-party checks rather than re-runs of the authors' own code.

**What was not done, and why.** No script in the author repository was executed. Both `uploads/` and the cloned repository were treated as untrusted input and read only; nothing was installed, deserialised, or run, and `main.tex` was not compiled. Consequently, claims about what `make_all.py` would produce if run are inferences from static reading of the code, not observations of its output. Where a finding depends on that inference — specifically, that `crb_analysis()` is never called because it is defined after `if __name__ == "__main__"`, and that three artifacts have no generator in the build chain — the reasoning is stated so the authors can check it against an actual run.

**What was not assessed.** The repository was cloned at depth 1, so the commit history could not be examined and no statement is made about when any defect was introduced, with one exception: the broken accent escape in the Petchey entry (Minor 17) is attributed to this round because the archived pre-revision bibliography contains the correct form. Journal formatting compliance (previous Minor 8) could not be assessed without the journal's template. The efficiency and numerical stability of the root-finding implementation were not profiled.

**Where literature search was incomplete.** The paid academic-search route returned poor recall on this topic — four queries on epidemic forecast horizons and Cramér–Rao bounds returned unrelated material on seismic prediction, MRI spectroscopy, and ICU rehabilitation — and two full-text deep reads failed (an upstream OCR error for Drake, and a paywall for Park et al.), so no deep-read artifacts were produced. Verification fell back to Crossref content negotiation, EuropePMC, and general web search, all of which succeeded: all 30 DOIs among the 38 cited entries were verified against Crossref, and every attribution claim in Section 4 was checked against the primary source's abstract or full text. The one claim that therefore cannot be made is exhaustiveness of the prior-art search. Major 15 asserts that Wesselkamp et al. and Castro et al. should be cited; it does not assert that no other precedent exists. Both of those works were found in the authors' own `references_working_library.bib` and `references.bib` rather than through systematic search, which is itself part of the finding.

**On the distinction between fact and judgement.** Findings marked [verified] are matters of fact on which the authors have no room to disagree about the observation itself, though they may of course disagree about its significance. Findings marked [judgement] — principally Major 7 (the Cramér–Rao category mismatch), the cross-pathogen comparability of four different surveillance measurands, and the magnitude of the bias introduced by resetting variance to zero at each rolling origin — are methodological assessments where the underlying facts are verified but the conclusion drawn from them is this reviewer's, and the authors are entitled to argue otherwise. No finding in this review rests on speculation about problems that were not observed.
