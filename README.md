## 📌 Executive Summary (研究核心结论)

Adapting the ecological forecast horizon paradigm (Petchey et al., 2015) to branching-process epidemic dynamics, this study derives an additive decomposition of prospective relative mean squared error under assumptions (A1)–(A4):

$$\text{relMSE}^2(h) = \underbrace{\text{CV}^2(h)}_{\text{Demographic Stochastic Floor}} + \underbrace{P(h)}_{\text{Parameter Amplification}} \le \tau^2$$

The empirical layer uses a three-tier design; all numbers below are generated by the build chain in this repository.

1. **Micro transmission chains validate the generating assumption.** Negative-binomial maximum-likelihood fits to two individual-level transmission-chain datasets (Hong Kong COVID-19, Adam et al. 2020; Guinea Ebola, Faye et al. 2015) reject Poisson overwhelmingly (ΔAIC 93.9–244.0), with dispersion estimates k̂ = 0.11–0.43 in the published range. The bootstrap sampling variance of R̂ relative to the Cramér–Rao bound is 0.98–1.06 — an individual-level, non-circular check of Theorem 4.
2. **State-level panels expose an aggregation bias in horizons.** Across 51 US jurisdictions and seven epidemic phases (COVID-19, influenza, RSV; NHSN weekly hospital admissions via the three CDC forecast hubs), state-level median horizons of 4.5–10.9 weeks are shorter than the national aggregates (7.9–67.8 weeks) in six of the seven phases (the exception is Omicron, where the two are essentially equal). Reporting national-aggregate horizons is therefore optimistic; operational decisions should use the state-level distribution.
3. **Four-term error accounting, executed per state.** The unattributed structural residual has a median share of 21%–97% and rises with horizon (e.g., Delta 74.8% at h=1 to 97.0% at h=4), positive in 62%–100% of states and dominating observed error. Short-horizon low-count configurations with negative residuals are reported unclipped as calibration-band diagnostics.
4. **Two-tier, exploratory framework with an external benchmark.** Per-state rolling backtests put operational skill against a persistence baseline at 1.0–4.3 weeks (state medians; national 1.0–5.3 weeks). The official COVIDhub-ensemble forecasts degrade to parity within 1–2 weeks as well (11 of 12 origins cross at a median of 1.0–1.4 weeks; one does not cross within four weeks), ruling out insufficient tuning of our own model as the explanation.

## 🔁 One-command replication

```bash
python scripts_v2/make_all.py          # full chain, ~5–7 min
python scripts_v2/make_all.py --fast   # skip theorem simulations, ~3 min
```

The chain: data-integrity checks → state-panel parameter estimation, horizon
root-finding and rolling backtests → four-term error accounting → LaTeX table
bodies → empirical figures → theorem/Corollary Monte-Carlo verification →
Section-3 figures → consistency tests (build fails on any manuscript/JSON
divergence). Input data files are hash-pinned in [`MANIFEST.md`](MANIFEST.md)
together with per-stage runtimes and provenance notes.

## 📊 Data provenance

- **Micro layer.** Hong Kong COVID-19 contact-tracing cohort (Adam et al.,
  *Nature Medicine* 26:1714, 2020); Guinea Conakry Ebola transmission network
  (Faye et al., *Lancet Infectious Diseases* 15:320, 2015); Lloyd-Smith et al.
  offspring-distribution parameters (*Nature* 438:355, 2005).
- **Panel layer.** Weekly hospital admissions for COVID-19, influenza and RSV
  for 51 state-level US jurisdictions, obtained from the terminal target data of
  the COVID-19 Forecast Hub (`reichlab/covid19-forecast-hub`), FluSight and the
  RSV Forecast Hub. All three are NHSN weekly hospital-admission series — one
  indicator class throughout, with no cross-indicator splicing.
- **Benchmark layer.** COVIDhub-ensemble archived forecast files (12 forecast
  origins, Delta and Omicron waves) from the official hub repository.

`scripts_v2/ingest_and_aggregate.py` re-derives the weekly COVID panel from the
daily truth file, asserts structural invariants of all three panels, and pins
each deposited series by the national window sums used in the manuscript.

## ✅ Consistency guarantees

`check_consistency.py` implements eight assertion classes and halts the build on
any failure: (1) every table fragment regenerated from the JSONs and compared
byte-for-byte; (2) every referenced figure exists and is non-blank; (3) a
stale-literal blacklist scanned across all reader-facing files (`main.tex` and
this README); (4) abstract headline ranges matched to the JSONs; (5) the
micro-layer bootstrap/CRB ratios matched to the fit JSON; (6) a dimensional
assertion that every error-budget drift row equals h_week × the per-week drift
variance; (7) two-sided crossing assertions for the rolling evaluation; and
(8) deposited-series identity pins for all three panels.
